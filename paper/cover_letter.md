Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “When Does Recalibration Help
Cellular Throughput Commitments? Accounting for Cost, Missingness, and
Abstention,” formatted in two-column `IEEEtran` journal style for
consideration in *IEEE Transactions on Network and Service Management*.

Centered on a practical empirical accounting methodology, reporting
checklist, and reusable open-source CLI tool (`audit_policy.py`), the
manuscript investigates when budgeted conformal recalibration helps or
misleads cellular throughput commitments across 16 retrospective
physical campaign-dates and 26 additional dates from held-out public
archives. We report explicit cluster power and TOST equivalence analyses
alongside all negative results: on 16 retrospective campaign-dates,
utility gains exceeding $`\pm 2`$ percentage points are unlikely
($`\mathrm{MDE}_{80}=1.65`$ pp, $`p_{\text{TOST}}=0.0018`$), conditional
upper violation risks span $`9\%`$–$`68\%`$ across mobile cohorts well
above nominal $`\alpha=0.10`$, supported measured throughput stays
around $`1\%`$–$`3\%`$, aggregate contrasts depend heavily on
offer-count pooling weights, and sparse finite-sample bounds become
vacuous on $`36/40`$ evaluated cells.

All evidence is retrospective from public archives and no live field
validation was performed. Repository source code is released under the
MIT License (`LICENSE`), and third-party public dataset terms are
documented separately without redistribution.

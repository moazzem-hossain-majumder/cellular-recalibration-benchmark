Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, “When Does Recalibration Help
Cellular Throughput Commitments? Accounting for Cost, Missingness, and
Abstention,” formatted in two-column IEEEtran journal style for
consideration in IEEE Transactions on Network and Service Management.
Centered on an auditable empirical measurement and causal-accounting
framework, the manuscript investigates when budgeted conformal
recalibration helps or misleads cellular throughput commitments across
16 physical retrospective campaign-dates and 26 additional physical
dates from held-out public archives. We retain all negative and null
results transparently: equal-cluster matched-cost utility differences
between reactive and fixed-cadence policies cross zero ($`-0.09`$
percentage points), conditional upper violation risks span roughly
17–63% across mobile cohorts well above nominal $`\alpha = 0.10`$,
supported measured throughput generally stays around 1–3%, and aggregate
comparator contrasts depend strongly on offer-count pooling weights and
become vacuous under sparse query budgets ($`B_1 \approx 6.8/K`$).
Repository source code is released under the MIT License (`LICENSE`),
third-party raw dataset terms are documented separately without
redistribution, and `prospective_trips_collected` remains zero pending
live field trials.

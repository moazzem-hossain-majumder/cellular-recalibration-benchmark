When Does Recalibration Help Cellular Throughput Commitments? Accounting for Cost, Missingness, and Abstention

Md. Moazzem Hossain Majumder

Abstract

Across six retrospective cohorts and four held-out public archives evaluated under frozen tree predictors and fixed policies, mobile conditional upper violation risk spans 9% to 68%—far above nominal α=0.10\alpha=0.10—while supported measured throughput remains 1% to 3%, forfeiting most achievable capacity. Why do nominally matched controllers often appear safer in conventional evaluations? Our principal contribution formalizes five necessary single-factor accounting conditions and empirically tests them via controlled ablations and an open-source accounting library and CLI (audit_policy.py). Unpacking all-issued risk via R=(M/N)R+R=(M/N)R_+, matching realized queries, bounding missing outcomes via [e/M,(e+m)/M][e/M,(e+m)/M], separating offer-weighted from equal-cluster pooling, and adjusting for campaign clustering systematically reverse risk rankings, flip an apparent held-out risk reduction into an equal-cluster increase, and leave ±50%\pm 50\% utility screening largely inconclusive across three campaigns per scope. Held-out scores remain a frozen retrospective evaluation with prior archive access rather than independent replication; all evidence is from public archives without closed-loop deployment.

Cellular throughput, measurement methodology, selective risk, cluster equivalence, sparse feedback.

Introduction

Across archived mobile cellular traces under frozen tree predictors and sparse recalibration, conditional upper violation risk spans 9%9\%–68%68\% above nominal α=0.10\alpha=0.10 while supported measured throughput remains around 1%1\%–3%3\%. Conventional evaluations built around point accuracy, all-issued error rates, or equal nominal probe caps obscure these failures because a controller can appear safer merely by abstaining more often, triggering queries inconsistently, or omitting censored horizon outcomes.

We ask: do nominally matched cellular throughput recalibration policies remain comparable once evaluated over realized measurement opportunities, finite-record missing-outcome bounds, and campaign-level dependence? Rather than proposing a headline recalibration controller or bundling unrelated benchmarks, our single primary contribution is establishing and testing exact conditions under which evaluation choices reverse controller comparisons on cellular traces, operationalized via an open-source accounting library, fairness/reporting checklists, and CLI tool (src/audit/recalibration_audit.py, scripts/audit_policy.py). We organize our evidence around four research questions across G=16G=16 retrospective and G=26G=26 held-out dates spanning six physical campaigns:

  1. RQ1 (Reversal conditions & ablation): Under what formal conditions do individual accounting layers reverse comparisons (Proposition 1), and how often do single-factor flips occur?

  2. RQ2 (Risk–utility & fair matching): Do adaptive controllers improve supported throughput UU per unit conditional risk R+hiR_+^{\text{hi}} once realized queries QnewQ_{\text{new}} and offer rates AA are jointly matched?

  3. RQ3 (Frozen case study & formal limits): How do MARUR and PACER behave as frozen retrospective case studies, and when do formal pacing bounds hold vacuously?

  4. RQ4 (Campaign precision & scope): What precision limits remain across three campaigns per geographic scope?

All evidence comes from retrospective public archives without independent external replication or live field validation, and it does not cover other devices, operators, measurement tools, or closed-loop deployment; all negative findings are retained.

Related Work

Cellular Measurement, Prediction, and Rate Decisions

Operational measurements establish that cellular throughput depends on radio conditions, mobility, and collection protocol. Earlier channel/context traces (Raca et al. 2020) and commercial millimeter-wave (mmWave) fifth-generation (5G) campaigns (Narayanan et al. 2020) enabled context-aware rate prediction, while newer multi-technology datasets and machine-learning evaluations extend these insights to non-standalone 5G deployments (Kousias, Rajiullah, Caso, Ali, et al. 2024; Caso et al. 2024; Kousias, Rajiullah, Caso, Alay, et al. 2024). However, forecast errors sampled within a single route or shared drive are temporally correlated rather than independent replications, and low point error does not bound rate-commitment violations.

Missing inputs and deployment realism motivate complementary lines of work. The Boosted Additive Model for data with Missing Observations (BAMMO) addresses missing input covariates in commercial 5G prediction (Jacobson et al. 2025), rather than failed acquisition of future calibration outcomes. Smartphone streaming traces expose real-time sampling shift (Raca et al. 2024), multi-carrier measurements reveal divergent link predictability (Basit et al. 2024), and CellReplay (Sentosa et al. 2025) solves trace-replay emulation drift caused by adaptive workload feedback. Even without emulation-workload drift on fixed logs, however, global, grouped, and adaptive conformal baselines such as Sirhan (Sirhan 2026) evaluate full-outcome splits without auditing probe budgets or missing completions.

Why Isolated Accounting Adjustments Are Insufficient

Existing statistical methodologies examine missingness, adaptation, or sparse labels individually: missing-pattern conformal prediction (Zaffran et al. 2023) and covariate-shift or weighted quantiles (Tibshirani et al. 2019; Barber et al. 2023) focus on covariate or smooth distributional drift; sequential time-series conformal methods (Zaffran et al. 2022; Xu and Xie 2023; Lin et al. 2022; Gibbs and Candès 2024), ACI/OGD (Gibbs and Candès 2021; Bhatnagar et al. 2023; Angelopoulos et al. 2023; Anastasios Nikolas Angelopoulos et al. 2024; Podkopaev et al. 2024), and risk control (Bates et al. 2021; Anastasios N. Angelopoulos et al. 2024; Angelopoulos et al. 2025; Gibbs et al. 2025; Gauthier et al. 2026) generally assume complete step-wise feedback; while upfront stopping (Ilyas et al. 2026), semi-bandit feedback (Ge et al. 2025), sporadic feedback (Hou et al. 2025), and selective abstention/stability analyses (Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017; Min et al. 2026) treat isolated aspects of cost or abstention. On real cellular logs, however, evaluation failures compound across layers: shrinking offer frequency masks conditional risk spikes while simultaneously changing realized cooldown queries, position-locked missingness interacts asymmetrically with periodic updates, and offer-count covariance across heterogeneous cohorts flips pooled contrasts. Missingness Mechanisms, Margin Choice, and Utility Meaning

Missing horizon outcomes arise from two mechanisms in the logs: trace or session truncation censoring past log completion, and synthetic deterministic masking (QueryCensor20), which suppresses every fifth eligible tick and selectively skips fixed periodic updates while hitting asynchronous triggers. Partial-identification bounds [eq:risk_bounds] (Manski 1989) require only binary bounded loss outcomes without assuming missingness-at-random, whereas supported throughput UU conditions on observed yty_t and cannot bound unmeasured sessions.

Because no standard cellular SLA defines a universal relative utility equivalence threshold, ±50%\pm 50\% (and ±25%\pm 25\% sensitivity) serve explicitly as exploratory precision-screening benchmarks asking whether three campaigns can rule out halving or doubling baseline throughput—not production acceptability thresholds. Fixed one-point and two-point margins likewise span excessive fractions of a 1%1\%–3%3\% baseline. Operationally, supporting only a few percent of throughput means conservative backoffs and abstention leave almost all link capacity uncommitted.

Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion

Implications for Cellular Evaluation Practice: Our findings urge four concrete changes in cellular adaptation evaluation: (1) report R+R_+, [e/M,(e+m)/M][e/M,(e+m)/M], and UU jointly rather than all-issued RR alone; (2) match baselines on both realized probes QnewQ_{\text{new}} and offer frequency AA; (3) pair offer-weighted contrasts with equal-unit and campaign-adjusted intervals; and (4) distinguish frozen retrospective case studies from independent external trials.

Limitations and Scope: All evidence comes from retrospective public archives across six physical campaigns (4242 dates) under frozen parameters rather than independent external replication or live field validation (PACER followed MARUR inspection with prior archive access). These archives do not cover other user devices, absent operators, alternative probing tools, non-60s horizons, or closed-loop congestion; reproduction runs CPU-only via scripts/reproduce_all.py and scripts/audit_policy.py.

Conclusion: Joint measurement-accounting and inference choices systematically reverse controller evaluations on cellular logs: conditional risk spans 9%9\%–68%68\% above α=0.10\alpha=0.10 while supported throughput generally stays around 1%1\%–3%3\%.

When Does Recalibration Help Cellular Throughput Commitments? Accounting for Cost, Missingness, and Abstention

Md. Moazzem Hossain Majumder

Abstract

Calibration policies evaluated at the same nominal query cap can mislead through unequal probe spending, unobserved outcomes, and heavy abstention. Across 16 retrospective physical campaign-dates, matched-cost supported-throughput differences between Missingness-Aware Recalibration under Uncertainty and Risk (MARUR) and periodic recalibration cannot be distinguished from zero; power is limited, and two one-sided equivalence tests indicate that utility gains exceeding two percentage points are unlikely given observed cluster variability. Across mobile cellular cohorts under frozen tree predictors, positive-offer upper violation risk spans between 9% and 68%—well above nominal α=0.10\alpha=0.10—while supported measured throughput remains around 1% to 3%. We contribute an open-source empirical accounting framework and command-line audit tool (audit_policy.py) alongside a practical reporting checklist integrating realized probe attempts, finite-record missing-outcome risk intervals, selective offer decomposition R=(M/N)R+R=(M/N)R_+, physical-cluster equivalence inference, and pooling-weight checks. Benchmarking MARUR, Fixed-Period recalibration, and Horizon-Paced Selective-Offer Conformal Control (PACER) across retrospective and held-out archives shows that sparse feedback rarely cures out-of-domain failures, and all evidence remains strictly retrospective from public archives without live field validation.

Introduction

An application committing to a minimum cellular throughput requires useful delivered bandwidth alongside tolerable failure rates. Point error, low all-issued violations, and equal nominal recalibration caps do not establish either property: under sparse feedback, a controller can appear safer simply by abstaining more often, exhausting probes early, spending unequal realized queries, or omitting unmeasured outcomes from failure counts.

We address an empirical measurement question: when does recalibration feedback help cellular throughput commitments, and how do unequal query cost, missingness, and abstention make apparent gains misleading? Alongside our multi-cohort findings, our primary practical contribution is an open-source accounting library and CLI tool (src/audit/recalibration_audit.py, scripts/audit_policy.py) verified across six retrospective cohorts and four held-out public archives:

  1. Causal ledger and reporting CLI: Event-level separation of probe requests, horizon missingness, and finite-record bounds [e/M,(e+m)/M][e/M,(e+m)/M] satisfying R=(M/N)R+R=(M/N)R_+, paired with an automated warning CLI and practitioner checklist.

  2. Physical-cluster power and equivalence: Matched-cost contrasts grouped by campaign-date (G=16G=16 retrospective; G=26G=26 held-out), reporting 80%-power Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) and Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) rather than misinterpreting small-sample null intervals.

  3. Comparator and bound auditing: Benchmarking Missingness-Aware Recalibration under Uncertainty and Risk (MARUR) and Horizon-Paced Selective-Offer Conformal Control (PACER) against standard baselines, exposing pooling-weight artifacts and vacuous small-budget regimes.

All evidence is retrospective from public archives and no live field validation was performed; all negative findings are retained.

Related Work

Cellular Measurement, Prediction, and Rate Decisions

Operational measurements establish that cellular throughput depends on radio conditions, mobility, and collection protocol. Earlier channel/context traces (Raca et al. 2020) and commercial millimeter-wave (mmWave) fifth-generation (5G) campaigns (Narayanan et al. 2020) enabled context-aware rate prediction, while newer multi-technology datasets and machine-learning evaluations extend these insights to non-standalone 5G deployments (Kousias, Rajiullah, Caso, Ali, et al. 2024; Caso et al. 2024; Kousias, Rajiullah, Caso, Alay, et al. 2024). However, forecast errors sampled within a single route or shared drive are temporally correlated rather than independent replications, and low point error does not bound rate-commitment violations.

Missing inputs and deployment realism motivate complementary lines of work. The Boosted Additive Model for data with Missing Observations (BAMMO) handles missing covariates during commercial 5G throughput modeling (Jacobson et al. 2025), addressing unobserved inputs rather than unsuccessful acquisition of future calibration outcomes. Smartphone video streaming studies highlight real-time sampling shift (Raca et al. 2024), multi-carrier experiments document conflicting rate histories across links (Basit et al. 2024), and Sirhan (Sirhan 2026) compares global, regime-conditioned, and adaptive conformal intervals for mobile 5G rate selection. None of these studies audits how unequal probe spending, failed outcome horizon completions, and heavy abstention jointly distort controller comparisons.

Missingness, Adaptation, and Budgeted Feedback

Conformal calibration with missing covariates separates marginal coverage from missingness-pattern conditioning (Zaffran et al. 2023), whereas covariate-shift reweighting (Tibshirani et al. 2019) and non-exchangeable weighted quantiles (Barber et al. 2023) rely on structural weights that degrade under unmodeled drift and outcome censoring. Sequential time-series conformal methods (Zaffran et al. 2022; Xu and Xie 2023; Lin et al. 2022; Gibbs and Candès 2024), Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) and online variants (Gibbs and Candès 2021; Bhatnagar et al. 2023; Angelopoulos et al. 2023; Anastasios Nikolas Angelopoulos et al. 2024; Podkopaev et al. 2024), and risk-controlling formulations (Bates et al. 2021; Anastasios N. Angelopoulos et al. 2024; Angelopoulos et al. 2025; Gibbs et al. 2025; Gauthier et al. 2026) assume complete label revelation after each step, while selective classification (Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017) and interval-stability critiques (Min et al. 2026) formalize abstention–risk interactions.

Recent work examines partial or costly feedback directly: upfront stopping rules under i.i.d. arrivals (Ilyas et al. 2026), semi-bandit feedback revealed by set inclusion (Ge et al. 2025), and sporadic edge–cloud calibration (Hou et al. 2025). Our study complements these directions by auditing real cellular ledgers jointly over probe expenditures, unobserved outcomes, R=(M/N)R+R=(M/N)R_+, and physical cluster equivalence (Table I).

Decision Process, Accounting, and Audit Tool

At scheduled issue time tt, a frozen predictor outputs point forecast ptp_t from strictly prior lag throughput and radio telemetry. A controller selects nonnegative commitment Lt≥0L_t \ge 0 and decides whether to request outcome yty_t over the upcoming horizon (ready timestamp); ticks lacking a valid prior rate are marked ineligible (Lt=0L_t = 0). All controllers begin with identical complete initial pre-test labels excluded from new target queries QnewQ_{\text{new}} under budget cap CnewC_{\text{new}}.

Across NN eligible opportunities, let M=∑t𝟏{Lt>0}M = \sum_t \mathbf{1}\{L_t > 0\} denote positive commitments, A=M/NA = M/N the positive-offer frequency, ee observed violations (Lt>ytL_t > y_t), and mm positive commitments lacking observed yty_t. Because yt≥0y_t \ge 0 prevents zero commitments from violating, all-issued risk RR and positive-offer conditional risk R+R_+ obey R=A⋅R+,R∈[e/N,(e+m)/N],R+∈[e/M,(e+m)/M](M>0),\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} R &= A \cdot R_+,\qquad R \in [e/N, (e+m)/N],\\ R_+ &\in [e/M, (e+m)/M]\qquad (M > 0), \end{aligned} \label{eq:risk_bounds} \end{equation} where [e/M,(e+m)/M][e/M,(e+m)/M] are exact finite-record partial-identification bounds (Manski 1989). Whenever abstention shrinks AA, R≤αR \le \alpha permits R+≈α/A≫αR_+ \approx \alpha/A \gg \alpha. Service utility is measured via supported throughput U=∑t:yt obsmin⁡(Lt,yt)/∑t:yt obsytU = \sum_{t:y_t\text{ obs}}\min(L_t,y_t)/\sum_{t:y_t\text{ obs}}y_t.

Practitioner Checklist and audit_policy.py Tool

To operationalize these checks, src/audit/recalibration_audit.py and scripts/audit_policy.py ingest cluster-summary or tick-level comma-separated values (CSV) ledgers, verify [eq:risk_bounds], compute cluster bootstrap confidence intervals (CI), equal-cluster Student-tt intervals, between-cluster standard deviation (SD), MDE80\text{MDE}_{80}, and TOST pp-values, and emit automated deployment warnings (boxed checklist below). Run on our exported held-out cap-10 CSV (examples/worked_heldout_c10_ledger.csv), the tool automatically flags zero-offer clusters, unmatched realized queries, and offer-count pooling divergence (−23.41-23.41 pp vs. +0.37+0.37 pp).

Window, Nominal α\alpha, Source Predictors, and LOCO: Enlarging windows (W∈{20,40}W \in \{20,40\}) or varying α\alpha leaves PACER and MARUR conditional risks far above target across both retrospective and held-out archives without consistent equal-cluster utility gains (Table VIII). Similarly, replaying lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) source-only predictors—ThroughputResNet_57, QuantileGBM_p10_57, HistGBM_Clock112, Oslo pre-2010 OsloBus_QuantileGBM_p10, and Persistence—either retains elevated conditional risk or suppresses delivered throughput below 1%. Dropping any single cohort in LOCO preserves mobile median conditional risk near 47%47\%–49%49\% and median UU near 1%1\%; omitting Oslo Commute eliminates the negative held-out cap-10 utility shift without revealing positive gains elsewhere.

What Remains Untested: Four regimes remain unmeasured: (1) closed-loop live network deployment where rate commitments alter congestion and user Quality of Experience (QoE); (2) large query budgets (Cnew≥136C_{\text{new}} \ge 136) where Theorem 1 becomes tight at moderate offer frequencies; (3) cellular deployments outside European/US archives and correlated burst outages beyond QueryCensor20; and (4) non-60s prediction horizons independent of logged transfer intervals.

Limitations, Reproducibility, and Conclusion

Limitations and Reproducibility: All evidence is retrospective from public archives and no live field validation was performed. Random schedules inspect offline horizon eligibility, fixed 60-second clocks omit transport congestion feedback, and session-boundary/video-playback extensions appear in the supplement. All SHA-256 locked configurations run on Central Processing Unit (CPU) Python 3.11 via scripts/reproduce_all.py (with CPU fallback for Graphics Processing Unit [GPU]-capable ThroughputResNet) alongside CI checks and scripts/audit_policy.py.

Conclusion: Across 1616 retrospective and 2626 held-out physical campaign-dates, sparse recalibration cannot reliably rescue cellular throughput commitments: cluster utility power is limited at G=16G=16 (MDE80=1.65\text{MDE}_{80}=1.65 pp) with TOST ruling out gains above ±2\pm 2 pp (p=0.0018p=0.0018), mobile conditional risk spans 9%9\%–68%68\% above α=0.10\alpha=0.10, supported throughput averages 1%1\%–3%3\%, and sparse bounds are vacuous on 36/4036/40 cells.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Cellular Measurement, Prediction, and Rate Decisions}
Operational measurements establish that cellular throughput depends on radio conditions, mobility, and the collection regime. The channel/context dataset of Raca et al.~\cite{raca2020} and the commercial mmWave measurements underlying Lumos5G~\cite{narayanan2020} support context-aware prediction. More recent multi-technology measurements, longitudinal service analysis, and machine-learning modeling extend this evidence to evolving non-standalone networks~\cite{kousias2024data,caso2024,kousias2024model}. These resources support evaluation across conditions, but forecast counts within a shared route or campaign do not constitute independent replications. Moreover, prediction-error metrics do not directly measure the risk of promising an unsustainable rate.

Missing predictors and deployment constraints motivate complementary approaches. BAMMO incorporates missing covariates into additive boosting and variable-importance estimation on commercial 5G data~\cite{jacobson2025}. Its treatment of absent radio measurements addresses the input side of prediction; unsuccessful acquisition of future calibration outcomes is a separate problem. Real-time smartphone experiments demonstrate the importance of sampling, labeling, distribution shift, and application integration~\cite{raca2024}. Conversely, fine-grained multi-carrier experiments expose conflicting measurement histories and limits to achievable prediction accuracy~\cite{basit2024}. Together, these findings favor evaluation at a specified prediction horizon and operational objective rather than comparisons of headline accuracy across incompatible datasets.

The closest application-level work is Sirhan's comparison of global, regime-conditioned, and adaptive conformal throughput intervals~\cite{sirhan2026}. Conditioning on radio-access technology and handover state can improve two-sided interval behavior without improving one-sided rate selection; a global bound can yield a better utilization--stall trade-off. Thus, neither conformal throughput bounds nor decision-oriented evaluation is a new contribution here. Our question concerns how the resulting commitments behave when acquiring calibration feedback consumes a limited number of attempts, responses can be unsuccessful, and telemetry patterns change.

\subsection{Missingness and Calibration Beyond Exchangeability}
Conformal calibration under missing covariates explicitly distinguishes marginal validity from coverage conditional on missingness patterns~\cite{zaffran2023}. Partitioning a calibration sample by pattern can address heterogeneous errors but reduces the number of available scores. A pattern-specific quantile obtained from a small, adaptively collected window cannot inherit a coverage statement solely because its construction resembles conformal prediction. Similarly, likelihood-ratio weighting under covariate shift~\cite{tibshirani2019} and weighted methods beyond exchangeability~\cite{barber2023} establish results under specified distributional and weighting conditions, rather than under unrestricted temporal drift and outcome-dependent observation.

Time-series methods exploit temporal structure in different ways. Adaptive conformal aggregation~\cite{zaffran2022}, sequential prediction of residual quantiles~\cite{xu2023}, and temporal quantile adjustments~\cite{lin2022} evaluate coverage together with interval efficiency. Online conformal inference also studies arbitrary distribution shifts and long-run calibration~\cite{gibbs2024}. Their guarantees concern different sampling models and coverage targets: asymptotic conditional validity, longitudinal or cross-sectional behavior, and time-averaged coverage are not interchangeable. For cellular commitments, the relevant distinction is between missing input telemetry, a requested label that never becomes usable, and a label unavailable until its measurement horizon closes. Evaluation must retain these distinctions instead of removing unresolved outcomes from the denominator.

\subsection{Online Adaptation and Limited Feedback}
Adaptive conformal inference adjusts the nominal error level using observed mistakes~\cite{gibbs2021}. Strongly adaptive online learning emphasizes performance over local time intervals~\cite{bhatnagar2023}; conformal PID control uses feedback and score forecasting~\cite{pid2023}; decaying step sizes connect retrospective coverage with stable-distribution quantile estimation~\cite{angelopoulos2024online}; and betting-based adaptation reduces manual learning-rate selection~\cite{podkopaev2024}. These developments provide substantive methodological baselines. However, replacing their feedback stream with selectively requested, delayed, or imputed scores changes the assumptions and does not preserve their guarantees automatically.

Label efficiency and partial feedback have also been studied directly. Ilyas et al.~\cite{ilyas2026} balance upfront calibration-label cost against prediction-set efficiency using a stopping rule for independently and identically distributed arrivals. Ge et al.~\cite{ge2025} analyze stochastic online conformal prediction when the label is revealed through inclusion in the selected prediction set. Hou et al.~\cite{hou2025} calibrate probabilistic linear-system solvers using sporadic cloud feedback and examine coverage, set size, and cloud usage. Therefore, adaptive querying, sparse feedback, and calibration-cost trade-offs are established research directions. The cellular setting considered here differs in its physical measurement attempts, unsuccessful responses, delayed admission of scores, and one-sided throughput commitments. These differences motivate an explicit event ledger and matched realized-cost evaluation; they do not by themselves establish algorithmic novelty or a new theoretical guarantee.

\subsection{Selective Risk and Decision-Oriented Evaluation}
Selective classification formalizes the trade-off between accepting predictions and reducing conditional error~\cite{geifman2017}. Risk-controlling prediction sets~\cite{bates2021} and conformal risk control~\cite{angelopoulos2024risk} extend calibration beyond a single coverage event, subject to their respective risk definitions and assumptions. Conditional conformal guarantees can target specified classes of shifts or subgroups~\cite{gibbs2025}, while adaptive coverage policies use a dedicated statistical construction to choose example-dependent coverage levels~\cite{gauthier2026}. A heuristic abstention gate is not equivalent to any of these constructions.

Metric choice is consequential. Min et al.~\cite{min2026} demonstrate that coverage and average interval length can conceal undesirable prediction behavior. For nonnegative throughput and zero-rate abstention, an analogous accounting distinction is elementary but essential: with a common eligible-opportunity denominator, all-issued violation risk equals the positive-offer frequency multiplied by violation risk conditional on a positive offer. Lower all-issued risk can therefore accompany unchanged or worse reliability among actual commitments. Missing outcomes additionally require finite-record risk bounds, which are not confidence intervals or prospective coverage guarantees. Supported throughput, positive-offer frequency, abstention, unresolved outcomes, and dates with positive commitments must consequently be reported alongside risk and realized query cost.

\paragraph{Research Gap and Contribution}
The reviewed literature already establishes missingness-aware prediction, adaptive calibration, selective risk, and label-cost optimization. The defensible contribution of the present study is their operational evaluation under explicitly accounted cellular feedback constraints, rather than their combination under a new name. We examine whether a fixed, missingness-aware query/commit/abstain controller improves useful throughput at matched realized attempts without concealing risk through abstention or unresolved outcomes. The evaluation uses outcome-independent scheduled opportunities and distinguishes initial successful calibration observations, new attempts, successful responses, admitted scores, and updates used by later forecasts. Comparisons are reported separately by cohort and include adverse results. This supports an empirical systems contribution; a claim of optimal recalibration, guaranteed conditional risk, or consistent superiority requires additional theory or independent prospective evidence.

# Prior work, overlap and defensible novelty

The closest same-task comparator is Sirhan's one-sided rate selection on the Raca traces. The closest same-campaign comparators are the Rome measurement and throughput-modeling studies. Neither an offset derived from signed residuals nor a rate–violation frontier is an original algorithm here. The proposed contribution is an empirical measurement audit linking the forecast issue clock, which outcomes become observable, how many lower bounds are positive, and what fraction of measured throughput those bounds support on a common future period. Its strongest test reports when changing the denominator changes the conclusion and publishes individual decisions for inspection.

## Claim supported by the comparison

A benchmark contribution can establish how much risk and usable rate an observer can identify under a specified forecast clock, telemetry pattern and date split. The Ireland rerun directly checks published values; minimum/persistence alternatives show mixed results across settings. A post hoc maximum among settings that satisfy a test risk budget is a descriptive frontier. Deployment requires fixing a candidate on earlier data. The US check supplies independent interval metadata and one informative held-out AT&T date. Its six violations in 33 issued forecasts exceed the nominal ten-percent target empirically. Pooling them with Rome completed sessions or Irish next-row labels would erase a difference in the outcome definition.

The literature and experiments support an auditable combination of established techniques, a published-method reproduction, and a failure analysis on real networks. They do not establish a new calibration theorem, a universal gain in throughput prediction, or priority over every prior endpoint-bias study. Full titles and author lists for the existing manuscript references are in paper/references.bib. This is a focused comparison, not a systematic review with measured search recall.

# Positive-offer selection and submission decision

## Fixed analysis before running the selector

Use the two Rome clock ledgers. The first calibration date fits each model's existing ten-label signed offset. All remaining calibration dates validate candidates. The existing suffix is used once for reporting; it is historically known. Candidate commitments equal the ten-label lower bound multiplied by 0.25, 0.5 or 1 for persistence or the frozen tree. Treat an unmeasured positive offer as a possible failure. A candidate must offer positive service on at least 10% of issued validation opportunities and support at least 5% of measured throughput. These exploratory service floors are design choices, not operator requirements.

Compare two selectors on identical candidates. One maximizes validation supported rate subject to an empirical conditional failure fraction at most 10%. The other also requires a one-sided exact binomial upper limit at most 10%, Bonferroni-adjusted across the six fixed candidates at familywise 5%. Break ties by fixed candidate order. When none qualify, issue zero and record failure of the service floors. Always report the unscaled reference policies. Neither the binomial calculation nor its multiplicity adjustment accounts for within-date dependence or temporal shift; treat it as an optimistic independent-offer benchmark. No result here can certify field reliability.
## Results and interpretation

Neither selector found a qualifying policy in either regime. On E1 even the quarter-rate tree had eight observed failures and two unresolved positive offers among 81 positive offers. Its supported throughput was 3.28%. On E3 the quarter-rate tree had zero violations on five positive offers among 252 opportunities, supporting 0.22% of measured throughput. Its adjusted one-sided upper failure bound was 61.61% under independent offers. Both selectors therefore issued zero in the suffix and failed the minimum service criteria. Full-scale persistence and tree results are included alongside these outcomes. The tested family cannot establish feasibility; other policies or different prespecified service requirements could yield different results. Selective classification jointly evaluates rejection frequency and conditional error. Learn then Test frames calibration as testing a family of candidate rules with error control. Here the loss is exceeding throughput on positive offers, unresolved offers count conservatively, and a throughput floor supplements the offer-frequency floor. The exact-binomial calculation is a simple comparator inspired by this literature. Temporal dependence prevents interpreting it as a field risk guarantee. Even under independent positive offers, zero failures require at least 29 offers for a one-sided 95% upper bound of 10%, or 46 offers after the six-way adjustment. The expression is the ceiling of log(alpha) divided by log(0.9). Calendar-date replication is a separate requirement.

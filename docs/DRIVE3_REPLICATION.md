# US Drive 3 replication

## Question and protocol

Would the frozen Rome tree and a persistence baseline give the same operational impression when judged on every issued forecast and on the subset promising a positive rate? We selected the unused Drive 3 and all three released carriers before scoring. The [analysis protocol](DRIVE3_PROTOCOL.md) records the models, split, denominators and intervention. This is a retrospective replication on an independently collected measurement campaign. The local protocol has no external timestamp.

The tree was deterministically fitted using the original 57 Rome sessions, original feature encoding and hyperparameters. No US labels trained the tree. The baseline forecasts the previous rate. Within each carrier, each model subtracts its own maximum signed error among the same first ten complete calibration pairs, clipping at zero. The radio intervention suppresses the three NR fields and updates their availability indicators, retaining the full-input tree offset. A strictly positive offer is counted even when its outcome is missing. A violation is an offer above the observed rate. Supported throughput is the sum of the smaller of each offer and its measured outcome, divided by the sum of measured outcomes.

## Data and alignment

Pinned release 782b712599f3710f7d3acff57097af6ca14660ae contains twelve processed Drive 3 downlink shards and eleven raw XCAL counterparts. The release lacks the Verizon day-four raw XCAL file. Those opportunities retain their measured rates and missing radio predictors. [Publisher archive](https://github.com/NUWiNS/pam2025-multi-carrier-dataset/tree/782b712599f3710f7d3acff57097af6ca14660ae)

Each issued tick requires a preceding measured downlink rate inside the publisher's transfer interval and preceding minute. The target is the last rate in the following minute, clipped at the transfer end. We aligned raw radio to processed records by exact microsecond device timestamp after resolving the logger's hour displacement by timestamp matches. The processed record supplies the publisher-aligned GPS clock. We excluded duplicate raw device timestamps and ambiguous GPS associations from the radio join. Every shard with raw telemetry had to pass a same-record raw-versus-processed throughput agreement check. The latest observed value of each signal in the strict pre-issue window enters the tree. The Rome scenario indicator maps to outdoor driving. Serving-cell handset and Rome logger measurements have compatible units but different instrument chains. The US instantaneous rate and clipped horizon differ from the Rome endpoint.

The first two publisher-GPS calendar dates supply the calibration pool. The final source shard crosses midnight on the UTC representation of that clock. August 9 contributes only two scheduled ticks per carrier; it is a continuation of August 8 driving. Carrier rows from a shared drive are paired exposures. They do not provide independent physical trips. Published intervals reveal starts and ends inside the roster, without identifying transfer attempts that never entered the roster. Positive commitments are evaluated against recorded throughput. No application actually acted on them in these measurements.

## Reproduction

Use the repository Python 3.11 environment with the original Rome active throughput CSV in its acquisition-manifest location. Run `python scripts/replicate_nuwins_drive3.py`. The script obtains and authenticates the publisher files, reconstructs forecasts and writes [all scheduled ticks and calibration partitions](../results/external_validation/drive3_forecasts.csv), [all carrier and date summaries](../results/external_validation/drive3_results.csv), and [shard-level alignment coverage](../results/external_validation/drive3_alignment.csv). Verify the rendered results with `python scripts/validate_manuscript_consistency.py`.

## Results

The three carriers supplied 897 scheduled ticks, 877 issued opportunities and 870 scored outcomes. All seven unresolved offers by the full tree and persistence had zero lower bounds, so the lower and upper all-issued risk endpoints coincide. On AT&T the tree broke 22 of 296 issued offers (7.43%) and 22 of 57 positive offers (38.60%), supporting 2.25% of measured throughput. Persistence broke eight of thirteen positive offers while supporting 7.29%. On T-Mobile the tree broke 123 of 294 issued and 123 of 236 positive offers (41.84% and 52.12%), supporting 1.25%. Persistence supported 71.55% but violated 201 of 294 issued offers. On Verizon the tree honored its sole positive offer across 287 issued opportunities, supporting just 0.0031% of measured throughput. Persistence broke all four positive offers. Thus the positive-conditional failure fraction exceeded the measured overall fraction on two of three tree cohorts. The Verizon tree recorded zero failures under both denominators.

Removing NR inputs at issue produced zero positive offers on all three carriers. This is a response of this fitted model with a fixed offset to the specified input change. The experiment measures no causal effect of switching off physical NR service. Across the twelve shards, 840,013 of 1,029,949 processed rows aligned uniquely to raw rows. The final Verizon shard alone supplied 104,447 processed rows without a raw counterpart. Matched comparisons and date-specific disagreement counts appear in the [paired-date ledger](../results/external_validation/drive3_paired_dates.csv). The test suffix is one shared trip; pooling carrier-ticks for a significance test would misstate precision.

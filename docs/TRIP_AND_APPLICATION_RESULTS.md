# Separate driving traces and trace-driven playback

## Frozen Rome model in Ireland

The original all-application cutoff had no eligible active driving downloads before it. The [protocol amendment](PLAYBACK_REPLAY_PROTOCOL.md) transferred the existing two-date calibration rule before calculating model results. December 14 and 16, 2019 supplied the calibration pool, with ten observed outcomes used per model. Eleven later traces span December 17, January 16, February 13–14 and February 27. One of 335 scheduled ticks had no prior rate. Eleven of 334 issued opportunities lacked a measurable full-minute outcome. Four missing tree outcomes had positive commitments. The same frozen Rome tree and persistence comparator shared all opportunities. LTE and NR signal strength and quality map to four predictors. Neither SINR field is available under an equivalent definition.

The tree violated 43 of 155 observed positive commitments (27.74%) and supported 7.74% of measured throughput. Depending on the four unresolved positive commitments, all-issued risk was 12.87–14.07%. Persistence supported 16.42% and met the 10% all-issued ceiling at 31/334 (9.28%), yet 31 of its 41 positive promises failed (75.61%). The preferred policy therefore depends on whether the service objective counts every scheduled offer or specifically the promises users can act on. These archived traces have previously been used for other project analyses. ## Norwegian application replay

The inventory covers 52 publisher files from all nine additional routes. Thirty files began before the fixed January 30, 2011 cutoff. The remaining 22 recordings span seven routes, seven calendar dates and eleven route-date groups. Each file is one recording; a calendar day can contain several routes. Different routes or files do not automatically imply statistically independent outcomes. This is the separate Norwegian lag model, since these recordings lack Rome radio fields.

Each client consumes the same cumulative service curve constructed from measured byte counts. Playback starts upon completing the first segment; stalling is the time with an empty playback buffer once playback starts. Representation bitrate weights completed segments equally. Each continuity break restarts the client, and two initial observations warm up both lag inputs. This produced 1,473 replay blocks per policy. Very short blocks and restarts limit field realism. Post-start stall summaries exclude blocks with no completed segment; there were 103 such blocks for the point tree and 17 for the calibrated tree. Report those counts alongside stalls, since sessions that never started have no post-start denominator. The client has no request overhead, buffer cap or encoding variability, and changing representation cannot change the recorded network service.

Median route-date stall fraction and delivered representation were respectively 19.12% and 1.013 Mbps for the point tree, 12.93% and 0.236 Mbps for the calibrated tree, 17.06% and 0.866 Mbps for persistence, 12.90% and 0.221 Mbps for calibrated persistence, and 12.30% and 0.150 Mbps for the fixed minimum. The tree's calibration decreased both stall fraction and bitrate in every route-date group. Its median route-date mean startup time fell from 2.52 to 1.10 seconds. These are marginal medians across the same eleven groups. User experience depends on the value assigned to uninterrupted playback versus resolution. No user-preference weighting was fitted. ## Reproduction and next evidence

Run `python scripts/ireland_drive_transfer.py` after the existing Irish preparation and `python scripts/replay_oslo_playback.py` after the existing Norway validation. The pinned [source inventory](../data/metadata/oslo_playback_sources.json) includes exact hashes and acquisition URLs. The full reproduction entry point includes both analyses.

A field test would require instrumented clients assigned policies on independently scheduled trips. Record actual segment bytes, requests, completions, player startup and buffer underruns, including failed starts. Freeze how stall and quality are valued before observing those outcomes. The present analysis can quantify a decision trade-off without establishing a live deployment benefit.

# When can a forecast be issued?

In the earlier Rome release, the first recorded row after a DQA completion starts the next candidate. Its lag is the last available mean download from the completed block. The endpoint requires a later completion. Completed, labeled sessions with an eligible lag are the scored population. Scenario is assumed known from collection metadata. In the later release the loader first removes missing or negative throughput, then uses the current measured fall to declare a new segment. Even the fact that the prior numeric reading is the final reading of a segment becomes established at that fall. An observer could detect a reset after seeing the reading. It cannot identify that same set of starts before seeing it.

The tabular model uses the preceding rate, previous duration, lag age, LTE/NR availability, their group, recent RSRP/RSRQ/SINR and scenario. The later radio lookback excludes the reset timestamp. The final endpoint and current duration are absent from the model input. This addresses direct use of the future label but leaves selection of forecast times dependent on the throughput path. A one-row segment can use the same reading to define the start and endpoint. The benchmark cannot identify prospective next-test performance without independent start and completion markers.

The clock replay in scripts/validate_clock_and_network.py schedules each whole local minute while the logger is running. A forecast is issued whenever the preceding half-open minute contains a nonnegative measured rate. The latest such rate predicts the last valid reading of the following minute. Ticks at a trace end or without a future measured label remain issued but unscored. A new measurement exactly at a tick belongs to the target minute. No interpolation or zero filling is applied. The forecast uses one lag to permit a matched comparison across endpoint definitions. The first ten observed labels of the existing calibration dates set the offset; suffix dates remain fixed.

On E1 the reset and clock persistence comparisons give V/U of 7.19/14.93 and 8.06/8.29 percent. On E3 the respective values are 20.39/51.65 and 13.82/16.00 percent. Clock scoring retained 211 of 238 issued E1 forecasts and 152 of 171 E3 forecasts. Missing labels could be informative. Endpoint, eligible population and calibration examples change together. The difference does not estimate a causal effect of the trigger. See results/diagnostics/prediction_time_validity.json and clock_forecasts.csv.

The earlier loader reads scenario and operator metadata from each block's closing row. An inspection of the raw earlier CSV found one scenario and one operator within each of the 45 campaigns, with contiguous campaign records and no backward within-campaign timestamps. These metadata could therefore be supplied at the beginning of the recording. The clock replay saves every issued forecast, including missing labels, in results/diagnostics/clock_issued.csv.

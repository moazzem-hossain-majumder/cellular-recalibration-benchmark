# Final research readiness

The MARUR experiment and required research documents are complete. The repository contains an executable empirical controller, causal acquisition and action ledgers, baselines, ablations, external replay, statistics, figures and a revised manuscript. The literature does not support claiming a new general conformal method or a universal controller advantage.

## What changed scientifically

The research now studies when to query calibration feedback and when to offer a rate under an explicit attempt cap. An unresolved queried outcome consumes budget and contributes a conservative residual bound. The full Rome clock roster retains 26 previously omitted no-lag ticks. Eight policies, three caps, two point predictors and two feedback scenarios yield 576 configurations across six cohorts. Primary opportunity construction uses time, not a future rate change. Historical reset-segmentation results remain supporting retrospective analyses.

On Rome E1 at cap 50, MARUR supports 2.02% and has all-issued risk 5.46%, compared with periodic support 1.01% and risk 5.88–7.14%. Costs are 50 versus 26 including ten initial labels. The same MARUR policy violates 13 of 29 positive offers. On E3 periodic support is higher and its upper risk lower. On Ireland the MARUR observed positive-conditional failure fraction is 12/155=7.74%, but the interval over all 159 positive offers is 7.55–10.06%. Positive-conditional target satisfaction remains unresolved at 10%. Increased throughput on US cohorts can accompany increased violations.

The missing-response experiment is synthetic controller-feedback censoring. Audit outcomes remain the same for paired evaluation. On Ireland under this condition, conservative residual completion supports 1.17% at all-issued upper risk 3.89%, while discarding missing responses supports 3.02% at 7.49%. Both pay for twenty new requests and receive twelve observed labels. The trade-off is recorded rather than hidden.

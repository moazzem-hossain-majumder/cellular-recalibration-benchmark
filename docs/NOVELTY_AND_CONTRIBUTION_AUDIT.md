# Novelty and contribution audit

The defensible contribution is an empirical acquisition-and-decision framework for cellular throughput commitments under delayed, unsuccessful and constrained calibration feedback. The working name MARUR does not establish algorithmic novelty. The previous 7.1/10 assessment was generated internally in this repository workflow; it was not an independent external IEEE review.

## Four aligned research questions

1. How do delayed and unsuccessful calibration requests affect selective throughput commitments? The causal receipt separates requests, completed outcomes, conservative missing-score admission, cooldown and later-used updates. Existing results describe the implemented fixed delays; they do not isolate a causal effect of delay because no delay-randomization experiment was run.
2. How does constrained acquisition change the reliability-throughput trade-off? The complete eight-policy archive holds predictors, initialization and opportunities fixed while reporting costs and both risk denominators. Feedback censoring is a deterministic position stress test and is confounded with periodic sampling phase.
3. Does equal realized cost change conclusions from equal-cap comparisons? The five-policy audit reports all 576 candidate pairs, identifies 384 exact-cost contrasts from actual queries, and retains unmatched references. Equal caps do not imply equal spending. Frozen can match only zero new queries.
4. Under what observed conditions does MARUR provide more service or sacrifice reliability? The full cohort/point-model/scenario/grid results and offer decompositions distinguish added or removed offers from changed commitment sizes. These are descriptive conditions in known archives, not a trained selector or general causal explanation of network behavior.

## What is original to this study

The concrete synchronized cellular replay, its acquisition and service ledgers, matched realized-cost audit, unsuccessful-query accounting and cohort-specific decompositions form the study's implementation and empirical contribution. The study demonstrates how conclusions change with the cost and risk denominator. This is original evidence produced by this repository; literature priority for any generic component is not established.

Primary pages and existing bibliography records were checked in the current and preceding audit. No new DOI or first-of-kind claim is introduced. Full assumptions must be checked before a future theorem or compatible head-to-head method comparison.

## Claims removed or limited

Do not call the framework a new conformal algorithm, an optimizer solving constrained utility, a guaranteed conditional-risk controller, universally superior, or a demonstrated source of physical measurement savings. Do not call supported measured throughput live QoE. The missing-score upper completion is simple arithmetic under nonnegative outcomes. No prospective collection was performed. No new theoretical contribution is established by combining existing components.

Remaining novelty limits are the lack of a demonstrated robust acquisition advantage, the absence of a theorem for the actual feedback process, and no directly compatible experimental implementation of the recent label-efficient stopping rule. The paper should compete as a focused empirical measurement and reproducibility study, with independent replication as its next evidentiary step.

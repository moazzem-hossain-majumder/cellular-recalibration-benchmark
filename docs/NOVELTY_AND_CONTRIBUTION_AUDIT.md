# Contribution relative to the literature

The Rome campaigns and their cellular measurement methodology were previously published by Kousias et al. and Caso et al. Jacobson et al. studied throughput forecasting with incomplete radio observations using BAMMO. Sirhan already evaluated lower conformal bounds, rate selection and radio-state conditioning on the Irish dataset. None of these components alone supports a priority claim. [Rome campaign](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCOM.011.2200707) · [Later campaign](https://doi.org/10.1109/OJCOMS.2024.3499370) · [BAMMO](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssc/qlae054) · [Irish decision benchmark](https://doi.org/10.3390/network6040092)

Tibshirani et al. studied weighted prediction under covariate shift and Zaffran et al. studied calibration conditional on missingness patterns. The benchmark uses elementary group offsets and empirically estimated weights. It supplies no new coverage theorem. [Covariate shift](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/8fb21ee7a2207526da55a679f0332de2-Abstract.html) · [Missing covariates](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zaffran23a.html)

The empirical contribution is the joint assessment of chronological label cost, supported rate and violations across Rome operator/campaign shifts, including how a measured endpoint or an independent clock changes the answer. Table II supplies the complete budget grid; Tables III and X expose dependence on the observational unit; Tables VI–VIII reproduce and probe the Irish comparison. The Norwegian analysis evaluates the global rule on a third country's measurements with different radio technology and no radio covariates. The evidence supports a reproducible measurement and benchmarking paper. General superiority, field QoS, a universal ten-label threshold and population-level reliability are unsupported.

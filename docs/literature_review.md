# Literature review matrix — Phase 1

Evidence checked 8 November 2026. This is a targeted scoping matrix. **F** = accessible full-text sections inspected for the stated comparison; **P** = abstract, repository description, or publisher-indexed excerpts inspected, with detailed experimental verification incomplete. F does not mean every derivation or result was independently reproduced. Findings are the authors' reported findings; limitations/opportunities are this review's assessment unless explicitly attributed.

Each row separates what prior work already covers from what could still justify our project. No numerical result below is a result of our own experiments. Unverified details are left unverified rather than reconstructed from secondary citations.

- [Springer: Unveil the unseen: Exploit information hidden in noise](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10489-022-04102-1): screened as adjacent uncertainty/missing-data methodology, not used as evidence of a telecom-specific gap.
- [Uncertainty-aware Traffic Prediction under Missing Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06800) was screened but not used to establish a cellular gap: similarly worded traffic literature must be checked for transportation-versus-network domain relevance.

## What the matrix does and does not support

It supports rejecting broad novelty claims such as 'first uncertainty-aware 5G predictor', 'first missing-data throughput model', 'first cross-receiver unknown-device detector', and 'first software-aware RAN anomaly detector'. It supports conditional, narrower investigation of cross-network calibration cost and independently validated sequential alerts.

It does not support a claim that any proposed method is new to all literature, that any baseline has been reproduced, or that a paper's own reported causal attribution is independently proven. Before Phase 2 freezes novelty, inspect full texts and citation chains for the chosen topic, including the October 2026 work. DOI strings were only supplied when observed in primary publication, institutional, or official dataset records; unresolved metadata remain marked.

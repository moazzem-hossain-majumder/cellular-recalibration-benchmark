# Baseline audit for the MARUR investigation

Audited on November 20, 2026 before evaluating the proposed adaptive policy. This audit reads implementation, saved decision ledgers, tests, manuscript and prior documentation. It preserves historical results. References to a passed baseline describe the existing artifacts and do not validate later MARUR changes.

## What the existing study establishes

The core empirical contribution is that lower throughput forecasts need three simultaneous descriptions. The observer must count when a forecast was eligible or issued, how often a positive rate was promised, and which promised outcomes remain measurable. On the primary fixed clock, the frozen Rome model fails the ten-percent all-issued target on E1. E3 achieves a low all-issued fraction while making only three positive offers on one date. The same model's transfer to US and Irish traces supplies scoped independent collection evidence. Neither the calibrated point predictor nor the underlying conformal order statistic is a new algorithm.

The repository already implements chronological source/target splits, frozen HistGBM and persistence, global and telemetry-group quantiles, every feasible Rome prefix, latest-ten daily recalibration, delayed score-offset feedback, complete-case and all-issued accounting, synthetic NR suppression, segmentation variants, three-carrier US transfer, Irish driving transfer, Norwegian route transfer, video replay, and a positive-offer feasibility selector. Adding these under one name would not independently establish methodological novelty. ## Segmentation findings already covered

The current reset implementation identifies a drop below -5 Mbps to a value below 5 Mbps; relative_drop uses -2 Mbps and 1 Mbps; active_burst uses upward crossing of 1 Mbps or a gap exceeding ten seconds. Cleaning is performed before segmentation. The saved audit enumerates all 43 files and both operator channels. Session counts are 584, 712 and 930, with 498, 626 and 844 causal forecast pairs respectively. The boundary audit also records zero-duration segments, gaps and multiple rising edges. Those facts show substantial endpoint dependence.

The downstream boundary comparison evaluates all extracted target instances with a source-only offset. It does not reuse the fixed suffix as an identical set of prediction opportunities across strategies. For reset, reported E1/E3 violation rates are 0.1479/0.2523; active-burst reports 0.4024/0.4918. These sensitivities concern both different endpoints and different rows. Repeating them unchanged adds little. An adaptive clock comparison can avoid this segmentation dependency by using the existing minute schedule and treating these analyses as supporting evidence. ## Data, statistics and application limits

The Rome source tree has 57 training sessions. The session analyses have two to eight evaluation dates per regime. Models and rules can overfit research choices even when individual training calls exclude future labels. Missing NR features indicate the logger's availability pattern and do not prove that NR service was absent. Forty-five of the 46 missing clock outcomes cross a recording boundary. A genuine service failure requires an explicitly defined attempted transfer and failure code, which these endings do not supply.

The US release includes multiple carriers but shared travel and instruments. The Irish driving extension has eleven test traces on five dates and two unmapped SINR fields. The Norwegian archive is HSDPA with no LTE/NR fields; its lag model tests a separate cross-technology protocol. Simulated video decisions depend on exogenous service, continuity-block restarts and the chosen bitrate ladder. Repeated replay settings or synthetic mask seeds add sensitivity, not independently measured application outcomes. [External provenance](EXTERNAL_DATA_PROVENANCE.md) · [Drive 3 limitations](DRIVE3_REPLICATION.md) · [Playback and Irish extension](TRIP_AND_APPLICATION_RESULTS.md)

The mean of per-date utility ratios answers a different question from a ratio of pooled sums. Both are valid descriptive choices when named. The positive-conditional failure denominator is itself affected by the policy, so a lower fraction without positive-offer exposure and delivered rate is incomplete. Binomial confidence limits assume independent trials; clipping weights, adaptive queries, censoring and chronological shift require additional theory before population guarantees are available.

## Material improvements to implement

1. Retain scheduled, eligible, issued, positive and unresolved actions in one timestamped replay, with explicit action reasons and actual outcome-availability times.
2. Introduce one small adaptive policy whose update trigger uses available history and current telemetry only. Keep a declared total label budget and distinguish request cost from successfully returned labels.
3. Match frozen, fixed-schedule, rolling/online and adaptive policies on predictors, clock, endpoints and feedback. Add ablations for missingness gating and the update trigger.
4. Report the entire prespecified budget family, all failures and abstentions, date-level paired contrasts and achieved calibration cost.
5. Implement temporal invariance tests that perturb later labels, preserve earlier actions, and verify that a label becomes usable only when its horizon ends.
6. Preserve the empirical risk-aware description until a theorem's assumptions cover the implemented label acquisition, missingness and temporal process.

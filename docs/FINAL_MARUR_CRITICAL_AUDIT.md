# Final critical audit of MARUR

Audit date: November 21, 2026. Scope: the local working tree of cellular-recalibration-benchmark, including the implemented controller, runner, validator, configuration, formulation, action/response/date ledgers and manuscript. This is a skeptical computational audit, not an external IEEE review or a certification. Original scientific results are preserved; added replay is exploratory.

## Verdict

The finite-record accounting is mathematically defensible under nonnegative, well-defined latent throughput and the stated opportunity roster. The controller is a heuristic with no proved conditional-risk, optimization or deployment guarantee. Its defensible contribution is primarily empirical, with an engineering contribution in auditable acquisition and decision accounting. Major Revision is appropriate for the current broad IEEE submission claim. See IEEE_SUBMISSION_DECISION.md for the scoring rubric.

## Formulation versus implementation

1. The stated constrained utility objective is not solved. replay has no objective evaluation, optimizer, minimum offer-frequency constraint, conditional-risk estimator controlling action, or optimization-based choice among recalibration/commitment/abstention. It selects queries using age and pattern and computes a signed residual quantile. The risk threshold is a nominal quantile parameter. The formulation now calls the objective an evaluation aspiration.
2. For alpha=0.1 and n=9 or 10, ceil((n+1)*0.9)=n. Thus the offset is the maximum residual, not a smoothly estimated 90th population percentile. With fewer than nine scores the finite-sample offset is infinite. The three-entry pattern gate is separate from the nine-entry grouped-quantile threshold. Rare patterns usually receive a pooled offset or zero commitment.
3. Replacing a missing score p-y by p is a valid upper bound when y>=0. For a fixed window and query schedule this cannot decrease an order statistic relative to the unknown true score. It does not guarantee smaller commitments than dropping the row: omission changes FIFO eviction, support counts and grouping. It supplies no population guarantee under adaptive acquisition, dependence or distribution shift.
4. Risk intervals [e/N,(e+m)/N] and [e/M,(e+m)/M] are finite-record partial-identification bounds, not sampling confidence intervals. N counts eligible issued point forecasts, M counts positive commitments, e counts observed violations and m unresolved positive outcomes. M=0 leaves conditional risk undefined. The endpoints assume a meaningful nonnegative latent outcome exists for every relevant opportunity; logger termination is not automatically a failed transfer.
5. Supported throughput is sum(min(L,y)) / sum(y) over observed eligible outcomes. It is a retrospective measured-service fraction, not live application QoE or utility over missing outcomes. It weights high-throughput outcomes and does not establish a causal deployment benefit.
6. ACI is a modified comparator: periodic paid queries, delayed feedback, a ten-score window, conservative missing-positive error, and a rate cap at p at EVERY alpha. The earlier wording suggested clipping only at an extreme boundary; it has been corrected. Original full-feedback ACI results cannot be imported.
7. New requests stop at budget exhaustion, but an already paid pending response can still update the window. The earlier blanket statement that exhaustion freezes updates was corrected. Simultaneous rows use the same residual state, with deterministic roster-order query reservation.

## Cost audit and schema

The original counter attempts starts at ten. These ten are successful historical calibration observations, whose failed acquisitions and measurement cost are unknown. Therefore neither attempts nor budget represents a true total measurement-attempt count. The configuration now calls its legacy values initial_label_plus_query_caps. The actual new-query caps are 0, 10 and 40.

Canonical summary fields now separate initial_calibration_observations, new_query_attempts, successful_responses, unsuccessful_responses, admitted_calibration_updates, effective_updates and initial_label_plus_query_count. For compatibility, attempts means the last hybrid count, response_labels means successful new responses, missing_responses means unsuccessful new responses, and recalibrations means admitted buffer updates. budget is the legacy hybrid cap. These aliases must not be described as total measurement cost.

Every new query is charged at issue, whether successful or unsuccessful. Admitted updates include conservative missing-score insertions except in DropMissing. effective_updates counts admitted new updates available to at least one later/equal-completion forecast, including no-lag scheduled rows; it does not count how many predictions changed. A final completed response can be admitted but never used. In dates.csv, response counts are attributed to the query's date, while effective_updates is a cumulative available-update count by the final forecast on that date; it is not a per-day increment. Do not sum that column over dates. Ordinary lag-rate telemetry, audit measurement, model training, failed historical acquisition and bytes/energy are outside the cost model.

## Trigger and ablation audit

last_ready and last_pattern always track the latest COMPLETED request, including an unsuccessful response. They do not track the latest attempted issue or latest admitted observed label. Pending-request exclusion already blocks another query before completion. Completion can be interpreted as a cooldown, but cannot be called evidence freshness.

Both MARUR and DropMissing reset those variables on failure. If a missing response has the current pattern, the pattern-change trigger stops firing and the age trigger waits sixty minutes from completion, even if DropMissing admitted nothing. A deterministic test with one failure followed by ticks 59 and 60 minutes after completion confirms the next request occurs only at 60 minutes. The policy is retained because switching to admission age would be a new controller, could cause repeated failed queries, and is not justified by a retrospective desire for better scores.

If future work needs evidence freshness, track latest_query_issue, latest_completed_response, latest_admitted_score and latest_observed_label separately. An admitted synthetic upper bound is not a successful observation. Predeclare any change to which timestamp drives querying and validate on new trips.

The DropMissing ablation changes only admission of unresolved scores; its subsequent window, support and commitments may differ. All 72 original cohort/model/scenario/cap query schedules are identical to MARUR. MARUR_Periodic changes request scheduling while retaining pattern gating/grouping, a valid schedule-component comparison. NoPattern removes pattern triggering, gating AND grouping together; it is a bundled pattern-component ablation, not evidence isolating any one of them. No extra controller was introduced.

QueryCensor20 hides labels from eligible ordinal positions divisible by five, not every fifth request. Under periodic sampling at positions 1,16,31,... it adds no censoring. Across original requested-feedback cohort/policy groups its synthetic censored fraction ranges from 0 to 40%. This is deterministic position censoring with schedule aliasing, not a uniform 20% query-loss experiment or a model of physical outages. All scenarios are retained and this limitation is explicit.

## Matched-cost findings and cohort interpretation

The added fixed grid has 0,1,5,10,20,40 NEW-query caps. It replays the full suffix with every original point predictor and feedback scenario and five policies. Comparisons are labeled matched only when both policies actually spend the stated count. There is no interpolation, truncation to an easier suffix, choice of a winning cost, or fabricated cost assigned to Frozen. Frozen can be matched only at zero new queries. Its reference remains visible at other caps with unmatched status. See MATCHED_COST_ANALYSIS.md and the complete 720-row ledger.

At the common ten-new-query point, recorded HistGBM results show:

- E1: MARUR supports 2.907% versus Periodic 0.523%, Early 0.495%, ACI 0.248%. All-issued upper risk is 10.924%, versus 6.303%, 5.042%, 2.941%. Relative to Periodic it removes four offers and adds 27, producing ten more observed failures. Greater service has a risk cost; the older unequal-cost 40-versus-16 result cannot establish matched-cost dominance.
- E3: MARUR supports 1.267% versus Periodic/ACI 0.605% and Early 0.065%, but upper risk rises to 15.789% versus 5.848% and 1.754%. It adds 30 offers to Periodic's 19 and all 16 additional observed failures occur on those extra offers. Compared with Early, both selection and shared-offer bound changes contribute. The frozen reference makes only three offers on one date.
- AT&T: MARUR supports 2.819% versus Periodic 0.237%, Early 0.131%, ACI 0.230%. Risk is 12.838% versus 1.689%, 1.014%, 1.351%. Relative to Periodic all 33 additional failures occur on 90 additional offers. Its positive-conditional risk of 37.624% remains unacceptable for a 10% service promise despite being below Periodic's 45.455%.
- T-Mobile: MARUR supports 0.038% versus Periodic 0.056%, Early 0.026%, ACI 0.053%. Its all-issued risk is 3.401%, equal to Periodic, while conditional risk is worse (62.5% versus 55.556%). It removes two Periodic offers without removing a failure. Against Early it adds six offers and six net failures; against ACI it has less utility and more risk. Pattern-gate abstention occurs on 166 scheduled ticks.
- Verizon: MARUR supports 0.550% versus Periodic/ACI 0.006% and Early 1.408%. Risk is 6.969% versus 0.348% and 9.408%. Compared with Periodic it adds 37 offers and 19 failures. Compared with Early it removes ten offers and six failures, with no failure improvement on the shared offers. That apparent reliability gain is explained by selection. All positive offers occur on one date. Frozen makes one honored offer and almost no service.
- Ireland: MARUR supports 1.129% versus Periodic 4.117%, Early 0.135%, ACI 3.292%. Upper risk is 4.192% versus 8.383%, 1.198%, 7.186%. MARUR and Periodic both make 159 positive offers on five dates; MARUR has fourteen fewer failures on their shared offers because commitments are smaller. This reliability gain is not increased abstention, but costs useful rate. Compared with ACI it adds 39 offers with no observed extra failures and removes 13 failures on shared offers. Relative to Early it supplies more service and more failures. Its finite-record conditional interval is 6.289–8.805%, which does not certify future 10% risk.

These are descriptive decompositions of deterministic replays, not causal explanations of radio conditions. Both point models and both feedback scenarios, every grid cost and every baseline are provided in the matched-cost report. The ten-query slice is a common audit illustration, not a selected deployment policy or confirmatory hypothesis test.

All-issued upper risk equals (M/N) times positive-conditional upper risk. Thus abstention can lower the first without improving the second. Low all-issued risk alone is insufficient. Seven Rome dates per operator share collection conditions, the three US carrier streams share one drive (three calendar suffix dates, including a short midnight fragment), and Ireland has five dates. Neither forecast counts nor matched queries create independent campaigns. No tick-level confidence intervals or pooled significance claims are warranted.

## Novelty assessment

- [Sirhan 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8732/6/4/92) already evaluates global, grouped and adaptive conformal throughput decisions and service-risk trade-offs in mobile 5G. MARUR's attempt ledger and failure accounting distinguish this empirical protocol; grouping plus rate decisions is not new.
- [Ilyas et al., ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/ilyas26a.html) studies costly labels and an optimality/coverage-preserving stopping rule under i.i.d. arrivals. MARUR's fixed capped heuristic has no comparable proof. Different feedback and shifting streams preclude claiming superiority without a compatible head-to-head experiment.
- [Ge et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/ge25a.html) studies semi-bandit revelation when a true label lies inside a prediction set. Requested cellular failures are a different observation mechanism; partial feedback itself is established prior work.
- [Gibbs and Candes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170) establish adaptive nominal-level coverage results under their online construction. Our selective delayed modified ACI comparator inherits no such result.
- [Geifman and El-Yaniv](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html) formalize selective risk and rejection. Reporting positive-conditional risk is an application of this established distinction.
- [Angelopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814) provide conformal control for monotone losses under explicit conditions. MARUR's ledger bounds are arithmetic identification bounds, not a new conformal-risk-control theorem.

Primary pages were checked during this audit. Combining these concepts does not establish algorithmic novelty. The empirical value is transparent demonstration of cost, service, selection and missing-feedback limitations across archived cellular contexts.

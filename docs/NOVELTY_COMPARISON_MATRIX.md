## Exact gap and smallest contribution

The defensible target is a causal acquisition-and-decision protocol for the available cellular measurements. It separates scheduled opportunities, eligible predictions, positive service, query attempts, returned labels and unresolved requests. A queried missing outcome contributes a residual upper bound based on nonnegative throughput. An explicit attempt cap remains binding when feedback fails. Global, early, periodic, nominal-level adaptive and pattern-aware schedules share predictors and initial labels, while every later query is chosen before its outcome.

The contribution is the implemented comparative framework and the empirical evidence about when its adaptive controller helps or fails. The literature above already solves broad versions of calibration, abstention, partial feedback and budget selection. The current controller's time/pattern trigger, FIFO window and support gate are engineering choices. No new coverage, optimality or priority theorem is claimed.

The central experiment must show actual cost as well as cap, label failures, useful rate and conditional risk. It must retain all policies, model pairs, budgets and adverse cohorts. If MARUR only lowers violations by eliminating service, or uses more actual requests for a gain, that limits its advantage. The missing-response ablation and same-trigger periodic ablation isolate components. All available suffixes were previously inspected, so the findings remain exploratory.

## Claims removed or narrowed

Do not describe the method as the first missingness-aware calibrator, the first adaptive budget selection, universally risk-controlled, or superior across networks. Missing NR fields identify a recorded input pattern. They cannot establish a physical handover or radio outage. The initial ten labels were acquired before this study's replay; their unknown collection failures cannot be counted as ten known successful attempts. Lag measurement feedback also remains outside the charged calibration-query budget.

A prospective comparison would freeze the controller and service criterion before collecting new trips, charge all instrumentation costs, and compare it with label-efficient conformal and stronger task-specific rate controllers under compatible feedback. Existing archived experiments can establish feasibility and expose failures; they cannot substitute for that prospective evidence.

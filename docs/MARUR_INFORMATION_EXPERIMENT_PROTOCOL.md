# Exploratory information-freshness experiment

Fixed before running this experiment. Existing cohorts have already been inspected; these results are exploratory and cannot be relabeled confirmatory. All original experiment outputs and model predictions remain unchanged.

Primary contrast: observed-label freshness versus completed-response freshness, with identical five-minute minimum attempt spacing, the same completed-response pattern-change trigger, the same ten-score window, the same conservative missing-score admission, and the same initial ten successful labels. Only the source of the age clock changes. Latest attempt, completed response, successful observation, and admitted score times are separately logged. A failed but conservatively imputed response updates the admission clock but not the observed-label clock. Events are processed only after their horizon closes.

Controls: original MARUR without added spacing, Frozen, Periodic, Early, and ACI. Secondary contrasts: window 20 versus 10 and 40 versus 10 within each freshness rule. Larger windows grow only through paid responses; they do not receive additional free historical labels. Pattern gating, pattern quantile threshold, nominal alpha, prediction schedule, point forecasts, and outcome definitions remain fixed. Because increasing the window also changes retained pattern support, this is window sensitivity, not a pure quantile-rank intervention.

New-attempt caps: 10 and 40. Frozen remains at zero realized attempts and is descriptive, not an equal-cost comparator to positive-cost policies. Comparisons are marked matched only when actual attempts are equal. Unmatched comparisons remain visible; no interpolation or best-test selection is used. Report candidate and reference costs even when both fall below their cap.

Feedback conditions: natural recorded missingness, plus independent timestamp masks and ten-minute outage-bin masks at probability 0.20 using seeds 42, 123, and 2026. Masks are generated on all scheduled opportunity timestamps before any policy runs, shared across models and policies within each cohort/seed, and independent of throughput. They affect calibration feedback only; archived outcomes remain available to the evaluator. Natural missing outcomes remain unresolved in evaluation. The masks are stress tests, not estimates of actual network outage processes. No query-ordinal censoring is used here.

Report utility (original supported-throughput fraction and supported Mbps per eligible opportunity), all-issued and positive-conditional finite-record risk bounds, positive offers, abstention, positive dates, query attempts, successful/failed responses, admitted and effective updates. Retain per-date summaries, action ledgers, response ledgers, and masks. Per-date effective-update counters remain cumulative as in the existing aggregate routine; do not interpret them as daily update increments.

Analyze every cohort and both fixed predictors. Do not pool mask seeds or correlated forecasts into independent-trip significance claims. Summarize strict utility increases with nonincreasing positive-conditional upper risk as descriptive joint improvements, plus trade-offs and regressions. Undefined conditional risk from zero offers cannot count as an improvement. No practical noninferiority margin has been specified, so no noninferiority claim is permitted.

Checks: backward compatibility against the original 576 configurations; future-label causality and mask timing; explicit missing-feedback timing fixture; query spacing; no cost overrun; complete summaries and matched-cost flags; window-only changes must leave query schedules unchanged. No controller is promoted to the paper based on these exploratory results. Independent prospective campaigns are required to confirm a selected policy.

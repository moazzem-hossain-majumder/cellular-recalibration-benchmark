# Chronological and paired comparisons

Rome reset sessions are nested within traces, collection dates, operators and campaigns. The two operators are measured in common environments. Minute ticks may share a preceding rate with a previous forecast's outcome. Seven test dates per clock regime are the units for descriptive paired calendar comparisons. The same dates and 409 issued opportunities enter both point predictors. The 30 Irish partitions reuse 83 traces; the separate Irish chronological evaluation uses 16 held-out dates. Norwegian interval rows are nested in logs on a single route and operator, with two bus and three metro test dates. These units cannot be pooled into independent campaign replications.

per_date.csv contains each date's violations, denominators, supported numerator and measured-rate denominator, paired by method and date, plus omit-one-date estimates. Deleting any single date left ten-label E1 tree measured V between 29.63% and 33.33%; in E3 the range was 0–1.72%. E1 daily tree V varied from 12.50% to 40.00%. Report rate-weighted utility after summing numerators and denominators. Calendar deletion is a sensitivity range conditional on the training sample and prefix. Two campaigns do not support campaign bootstrap inference. No p-values or population confidence intervals for clock ticks are reported.

**Rolling origins.** For each of the seven suffix dates use either the first ten calibration labels or the latest ten completed labels before that day's first forecast. Test dates do not overlap. Later origins may use labels from earlier test dates. HistGBM weights stay frozen. Each fit uses ten labels, but refreshing the window requires observing additional labels. At each date the same feedback history is available to both policies. The static policy ignores it. For the tree, latest-ten measured V/U were 4.74/1.08% on E1 and 13.16/2.17% on E3. First-ten values were 31.75/7.15% and 1.32/0.065%. The effect reverses across operators.

**Sequential feedback.** A unit-step signed-score offset update uses the observed violation minus 0.10 in Mbps. Only labels whose minute has finished may update it. Tied issue times share the same state. It receives up to 211 or 152 subsequent measured labels, in addition to ten initial labels. Measured tree V/U were 13.27/2.76% and 5.92/0.64%. This is an offset-update sensitivity, distinct from canonical ACI, which changes the nominal level. Its acquisition cost differs from a fixed ten-label policy. The recordings have no verified change-point annotations from which to infer recovery delay.
